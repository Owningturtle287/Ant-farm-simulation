GROW AT YOUR OWN PACE

Colony & reproduction

Egg laying and development are separate. Existing brood can still emerge when laying is off. Seed nutrition, care, moisture, and temperature continue to affect development.

At normal development, the model spans about 30 colony days under warm, well-fed conditions. Each colony day is compressed into ten minutes at 1×. Use higher time speeds to observe longer periods.

Water-table changes move gradually. Dry surface pools evaporate; workers can probe deeper for moisture. Food parcels and brood need their own space. Water remains in ants’ crops.

Changing the ceiling never removes existing ants. Higher ceilings are experimental; actual time-lapse speed depends on your phone.
LAYERS OF THE CLEARING

Terrain guide

Loose mound soil

Bare, coarse pellets outside the entrance. Carrying distance, slopes, and colony-specific preferences produce uneven mounds. Workers can re-excavate narrow tunnels inside deposited soil.

Rooted dirt

Dark soil under grass. Ripe grass produces finite grain kernels that workers can collect.

Diggable earth

Soft soil continues at every depth. There are no stone barriers or harder layers.

Rain & puddles

Rainwater gathers in local depressions. Each square holds at most two model units. Squares disappear as water is picked up or evaporates; rain adds new stock.

SOLENOPSIS INVICTA

A fire ant society

One reproductive queen and a range of sterile female worker sizes. Large workers are not a separate permanent soldier caste.

Workers weigh nutritional signals, brood contact, antennal encounters, local chemical trails, age, size, and experience. Young workers tend toward brood care; older workers are more likely to forage. These are tendencies that can change with need.

Nestmates recognize a familiar blend of contact odors. Returning foragers activate nearby recruits. Food and water trails fade; an unsuccessful trip lowers a remembered source’s reliability.

Food and water have separate crop bars. Workers fill their crops by collecting from a source or dry food cache. The queen can take a meal brought by an attendant. New colonies start with empty crops and caches. Grains stay separate in storage, and each brood item has its own bed.

During drought, workers can explore and excavate short downward moisture probes. Reaching an exposed water-table fringe creates finite, replenishing seepage. Fully saturated space is not a dry nursery. Adjust groundwater depth in Colony & reproduction.

Each antenna samples its own local chemical and touch cues. Workers compare left and right, cast wider when a trail weakens, and sometimes pause briefly to sample. Carrying food, soil or brood takes priority over wandering. Open an ant to see its current readings.

Use More → Disturb the ground at 1× to see a brief local alarm response. With no threat, large workers return to useful work or wait as recruitable reserves.

Narrow peripheral mouths connect to shallow foraging passages. The mound above them can contain its own tunnel network. New colonies generate the revised layout; established colonies keep their ground.

A research-informed behavioral approximation, not an exact ant brain. Timings, distance, nutrition, the population ceiling, and resource portions use game-scale units. Full mating flights, multiple-queen societies, rival colonies, and detailed neural/chemical physiology are not modeled.
